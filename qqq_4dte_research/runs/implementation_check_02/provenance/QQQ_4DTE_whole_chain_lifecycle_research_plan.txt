CONCRETE IMPLEMENTATION AND RESEARCH SPECIFICATION FOR AN LLM ENGINEER
Version: 1.0

==============================================================================
00. READ FIRST: WHAT TO BUILD, WHAT NOT TO BUILD
==============================================================================

Build one reproducible Python research system that studies the FULL QQQ options
chain for every eligible expiration, beginning when that expiration reaches the
specified 4-DTE entry condition and following the SAME contracts until expiry.
Include both puts and calls and every observed strike, not a selected delta band,
not only near-the-money contracts, and not only contracts with complete histories.

The primary deliverable is an interpretable, evidence-backed description of how
whole chains and individual contracts evolve. A secondary deliverable is an
out-of-sample-tested conditional forecasting model for those joint dynamics.
An execution-aware decision layer may be added only after those foundations pass
their release gates. This is NOT a redesign of the P50 optimizer, NOT a mandate to
choose a particular spread, and NOT an assumption that a profitable edge exists.

Required logical separation:
  DESCRIPTION: What did the observed chains do?
  PREDICTION: What distribution of future outcomes is supported given information
              available at a historical decision time?
  DECISION: Which permitted action has attractive expected net P&L under a
            validated forecast and explicit execution/risk assumptions?

Do not use the objective of finding profitable trades to filter away inconvenient
contracts, periods, costs, or outcomes during the descriptive research stage.
A result of "no reliable predictive advantage detected" is a valid research result.

User intent locked into this specification:
  U01. Primary underlying: QQQ. Do not silently substitute SPY or pool both.
  U02. Research all strikes and both rights for every eligible expiration.
  U03. Track each eligible expiration from 4 DTE to its expiry boundary.
  U04. Preserve actual contract identity while also studying changing chain shape.
  U05. Make mathematical relationships and uncertainty readable to a human.
  U06. Prevent look-ahead, survivorship, dependence, execution, and model-form errors.
  U07. Keep the eventual decision priority EV first and P50 second, but do not make
       the descriptive model a disguised strategy optimizer.
  U08. Use the two named data-contract files below as authoritative data guidance.

Read this document in order before implementation. Section 29 provides milestones;
Section 28 provides the acceptance tests. Do not start with machine learning before
implementing the source audit, clock semantics, cohort identities, and label tests.

Terminology:
  MUST / MUST NOT = non-negotiable requirement.
  DEFAULT = concrete proposed engineering setting, not an empirical market fact.
  OPTIONAL = separate capability that must not silently change the core study.
  BLOCKED = a missing prerequisite prevents the affected calculation.
  UNSUPPORTED = outside a deliberately validated product/model domain.

No claim in this document is a measured property of the user's actual archive.
The local Windows data directories have not been scanned for this document.

==============================================================================
02. STUDY QUESTIONS, SCOPE, AND ESTIMANDS
==============================================================================

An estimand is the precisely defined quantity the research intends to estimate.
Register each estimand before computing it. A result without an estimand and
sample definition is not a publishable research output.

Core questions:
  Q1. How do contract prices, intrinsic/time value, spreads, quote availability,
      and supported IV measures change over a 4-DTE-to-expiry lifecycle?
  Q2. How do those changes depend on INITIAL strike location, CURRENT strike
      location, remaining time, time of day, and information known at the time?
  Q3. Which common movements explain the joint behavior of calls, puts, and wings?
  Q4. How much incremental out-of-sample predictive information is present beyond
      simple maturity/time-of-day baselines?
  Q5. Which regions or conditions remain unobservable, weakly supported, or unstable?

Explicit non-goals for the initial release:
  - No claim of a universal causal "4-DTE law."
  - No proof of exact real-world dynamics or universal arbitrage freedom.
  - No automatic strategy optimization, live order routing, or account access.
  - No assertion that every listed historical contract is present in raw_theta.
  - No inference of market-maker/dealer positions from open interest alone.
  - No use of a risk-neutral pricing process as the empirical law by definition.
  - No claim of continuous-time barrier probabilities from minute snapshots.

Research population:
  All historically identifiable QQQ expiration series within the requested archive
  date range. Every series receives a coverage/cohort status, including ineligible
  and incomplete series. Default date range is discovered available coverage, not
  a remembered date range or a hard-coded historical count.

Default primary cohort convention: CALENDAR_DATE_4, defined in Section 03.
Optional alternative convention: TRADING_SESSION_OFFSET_4, reported separately.
The latter MUST NOT be merged into the primary population under one "4 DTE" label.

Default primary observation frequency: one minute, subject to source semantics.
Archive all available contracts. Optional additional intra-minute research is a
separate dataset/frequency profile, not a precision claim for minute observations.

Two separate levels of analysis:
  Contract-level: identity-preserving paths and outcomes for each actual contract.
  Chain-level: one expiration's entire call/put cross-section at one decision time.

Keep the sampling unit and inferential unit separate. A chain can have many rows
but still shares one underlying market shock. Expiration and calendar-time blocks,
not individual strikes, are central units for uncertainty and validation.

==============================================================================
03. EXACT DEFINITION OF 4 DTE, COHORT ENTRY, AND EXPIRY
==============================================================================

Definitions:
  E_date = verified contractual expiration date in America/New_York.
  T_E    = verified economic expiry/valuation boundary used by the product adapter.
  D0(E)  = E_date minus four CALENDAR dates.
  t0(E)  = first scheduled completed-bar decision on D0(E), under Section 07.
  dte_calendar(t,E) = E_date - local_NY_date(t), expressed as an integer date gap.
  tau(t,E) = max(0, epoch_seconds(T_E)-epoch_seconds(t)) / (365*24*60*60).

CALENDAR_DATE_4 eligibility:
  - D0(E) must be an actual eligible QQQ regular trading session.
  - The product/expiration must have existed by the relevant historical time.
  - t0(E) is calendar-defined, NOT moved to the first convenient valid quote.
  - Missing data at t0 is recorded as missing; it does not alter the cohort clock.
  - A non-session D0 gives NO_EXACT_CALENDAR_4_SESSION, not an automatic substitution.
  - Missing files on a valid D0 give DATA_GAP_AT_START, not INELIGIBLE.

Important sampling consequence:
  Some expiration weekdays may have a weekend or holiday four calendar dates
  earlier. The primary cohort therefore does not necessarily include every expiry
  weekday. Show counts by expiry weekday and exclusion reason. Never describe its
  results as representing all expiration weekdays without evidence.

TRADING_SESSION_OFFSET_4, optional companion:
  Start at the session four session transitions before the verified last regular
  trading session for that expiration. The start and expiry sessions are both
  included; this can produce five observed sessions. Record the actual calendar
  duration and never call it exactly 96 hours.

Time fields to store together:
  calendar_date_dte; actual_seconds_to_expiry; remaining_regular_session_minutes;
  remaining_session_count with an explicit counting convention; minutes_since_open;
  minutes_to_session_close; elapsed_calendar_seconds_since_t0; observed_bar_index;
  weekday; next_closed_interval_length; early_close flag.

Use a versioned historical calendar. Account for holidays, exceptional closures,
and early closes. Do not infer a calendar solely from dates having local files.
A missing file is not evidence that the exchange was closed.

Freeze cohort entry time independently of contract availability. Keep three sets:
  U_listed(t0): contracts verified by point-in-time listing metadata as listed at t0.
  U_observed(t0): contracts whose actual quote/trade evidence was available by t0.
  U_lifecycle: union of contracts observed at any time from t0 through the boundary.

U_lifecycle is a retrospective inventory, NOT an entry-time tradable universe.
A contract first observed later on the 4-DTE day is a late appearance, unless its
prior listing is independently verified. Appearance is not proof of listing time.

Expiry is not automatically the last available dataset row. Store separately:
  last_regular_observation_time;
  official_last_option_trading_time;
  economic_expiry/valuation_time;
  exercise_instruction_boundary when supported;
  settlement/reference-price time and source;
  final_observation_status.

Exact product times and rules must be historically verified. If missing, continue
valid pre-boundary descriptive analysis but mark full-expiry results BLOCKED.
Do not silently use 15:59 close as a verified official expiry price.

==============================================================================
04. TWO MANDATORY VIEWS OF EVERY LIFECYCLE
==============================================================================

VIEW A: FIXED-CONTRACT PANEL
  Key: exact contract identity plus observation/decision time.
  Freeze initial strike coordinates relative to the same cohort's t0 market.
  Follow the exact strike, right, expiration, deliverable, and multiplier.
  Keep a contract through missing quotes, zero bids, deep ITM/OTM states, and expiry.
  A missing observation remains missing. A verified terminal payoff is a separate
  event and must not be used as a substitute for an earlier unavailable quote.

VIEW B: MOVING CHAIN SNAPSHOTS
  Key: expiration cohort plus decision time; rows contain available listed/observed
  contracts and their CURRENT relative strike coordinates.
  Record changes in support, strike listing/observation, and quote availability.
  Reconstruct the whole supported price shape; keep unsupported regions explicit.

Examples of distinct questions:
  "What happened to the call initially at the money?" -> View A.
  "How did the at-the-money call price evolve?" -> View B, possibly different IDs.
  "What happened to the initially 5% OTM puts?" -> View A with frozen initial bins.
  "What is currently happening in the 5% OTM put wing?" -> View B with current bins.

Do not calculate a buy-and-hold return from a sequence that changes contract IDs.
A constant-delta or constant-moneyness trading series is a REBALANCING STRATEGY and
requires actual trades and transaction costs. A chart coordinate is not a trade.

New contracts:
  Add them to View B only from documented availability. Preserve their first-seen
  and, if known, listing-effective timestamps. Add their partial fixed-ID paths to
  U_lifecycle with an explicit late-entry flag; do not fabricate t0 prices.

Contract adjustments:
  Maintain effective-dated root/deliverable/multiplier records. Do not merge an
  adjusted contract with a standard contract solely because strike and date match.
  If a deliverable cannot be valued correctly, retain its raw history and label
  mathematical/EV modeling UNSUPPORTED_ADJUSTED_DELIVERABLE.

==============================================================================
05. DATA DISCOVERY, COVERAGE AUDIT, AND OPTIONAL ACQUISITION
==============================================================================

5.1 Discovery before reading all rows

Read SOURCE-OHLCV and SOURCE-OPTIONS. Scan real filenames and metadata. Construct a
source catalog keyed by dataset type, session date, expiration where applicable,
and source version. Record path, byte size, mtime, fingerprint, schema signature,
and validation state. Detect duplicate logical partitions as errors.

Do not assume the archive is a "4-DTE archive" from a folder or mapping filename.
A file collected when an expiration had 4 DTE does not imply its 3/2/1/0-DTE
continuations exist. Explicitly test every expected session-expiration pair.

Build an expected-vs-present matrix across:
  candidate expiration; cohort start; every intervening trading session;
  options greeks/quotes; option trades; option OHLCV; prior OI; underlying OHLCV;
  contract reference metadata; terminal reference evidence.

Candidate expiration discovery has two levels:
  ARCHIVE_DISCOVERED: expirations observed in existing local partitions.
  REFERENCE_VERIFIED: expirations verified against a historical listing calendar.
Do not claim completeness across all historical expirations using only the first.

5.2 Historical contract inventory

A union of contracts with observations is not a complete historical listing file.
Use available point-in-time contract/reference records to distinguish:
  VERIFIED_LISTED; OBSERVED_BUT_LISTING_TIME_UNKNOWN; VERIFIED_NOT_YET_LISTED;
  LISTED_NO_OBSERVATION; UNKNOWN_UNIVERSE.

Do not infer "unlisted" from absence of quotes or "listed at t0" from a later quote.
If the denominator of listed contracts is unknown, listed-coverage percentage is
NULL with reason UNVERIFIED_LISTED_UNIVERSE. An observed-universe percentage is a
different metric and must be labeled accordingly.

5.3 Optional acquisition / repair

Default mode is LOCAL_READ_ONLY. The implementation must create a missing-data
request manifest; it must not silently download, overwrite, or manufacture data.
An explicit acquisition mode may fulfill this manifest under SOURCE-OPTIONS /
THETADATA FETCH / REPAIR CONTRACT.

Each request unit identifies symbol, dataset type, session, expiration, both rights,
ALL strikes, expected session interval, source version, and expected schema.
Use the contract's full-day single-shot Greeks procedure and documented fallback;
do not chunk and stitch a supposedly complete Greeks partition. Preserve atomic
writes, validation, restartability, retry handling, and unresolved-status reporting.
Verify installed API/plan capabilities when implementing the adapter; the historical
endpoint descriptions in SOURCE-OPTIONS are not proof of current entitlements.

If a service response is capped, truncated, paginated, or only returns traded/quoted
contracts, identify that limitation. An HTTP success code does not prove full-chain
coverage. Record response completeness evidence, not just nonempty row counts.

The contract's approximately 98% full-session integrity rule concerns the expected
partition/session delivery. It must not become a rule that every individual strike
must quote in 98% of minutes. Naturally sparse contracts remain in the inventory.
Do not force missing individual contract-minutes into the data to satisfy it.

Missing underlying sessions are handled only under SOURCE-OHLCV / EXTENDING /
REPAIRING THE DATASET. That file does not establish a canonical external downloader.
Do not use embedded Theta underlying_price to repair the authoritative source.

5.4 Additional contextual data

Historical rates, announced dividends, corporate actions, session calendars,
listing metadata, and scheduled-event calendars may be required by specific stages.
Their adapters must declare source, timestamp semantics, effective dates, and
historical availability. Do not invent a provider, an announcement time, or data.
Absent auxiliary data must disable the dependent calculation, not the entire raw
price/coverage study. Additional acquisition must be an explicit configured action.

==============================================================================
07. TIME SEMANTICS, AVAILABILITY, AND NO-LOOKAHEAD
==============================================================================

This is a release-critical section. Follow both source contracts' New York-time
semantics. Keep local NY wall-clock fields authoritative for sessions and joins;
UTC/epoch fields may be auxiliary for absolute durations, never a silent replacement.
Interpret naive source timestamps according to the named contracts, not as UTC.

7.1 Separate four meanings of time
  source_time: timestamp written in the source row.
  event_time: when the economic observation/event occurred, when identifiable.
  available_at: earliest time the observation may enter a causal calculation.
  decision_time: time at which a hypothetical user/model acts on available data.

An archived row's existence today does not mean it was available at a past decision.
Every feature must satisfy available_at <= decision_time under its clock convention.

7.2 Resolve minute bar labeling before using close/high/low/volume

The canonical contracts specify filtering and revealed-bar behavior, but an adapter
must still verify whether the physical underlying row label denotes the start of a
minute, its end, or an already-completed observation. Record this interpretation.
Do not guess solely from a filename or the presence of a 09:30 row.

START_LABELED example:
  Bar labeled 09:30 summarizes [09:30,09:31).
  Full OHLCV is available at 09:31, not at 09:30.
  At the 09:31 decision, include eligible option events with timestamp < 09:31.
  The user-facing revealed cursor may remain 09:30, but internal decision time is
  09:31 and must be used for actual elapsed time and execution ordering.

END_LABELED or ALREADY_COMPLETED input:
  Map source label to bar interval and availability based on verified evidence;
  do not add an extra minute automatically. Then apply the same causal rules.

If the labeling cannot be established, raw inventory/quality checks may proceed,
but causal feature, return, and execution studies are BLOCKED_TIMESTAMP_SEMANTICS.

7.3 Snapshot construction

For a fully revealed minute cursor b, apply SOURCE-OPTIONS' permitted subminute
inclusion only to events strictly earlier than b + one minute. For event-time
requests, use only eligible events at or before the explicitly defined cutoff.
Preserve raw timestamps before any flooring. Never join by floored minute alone
when that could introduce a later event into an earlier decision.

Within each contract, choose the last valid event using timestamp and source
sequence when available. Preserve invalid/crossed observations for audit rather
than replacing them invisibly. No prior-session quote becomes a current quote.

Use SOURCE-OHLCV for spot. For minute research at a completed-bar decision, use the
latest legitimately revealed underlying close, record its age, and record the
option-underlying timing discrepancy. Do not imply intra-minute synchronization
when the underlying source is only minute-level.

7.4 Quote age and observation age

Some interval rows may carry a sampling timestamp rather than the original quote
update timestamp. Distinguish last_quote_update_age from snapshot_record_age.
When the former is unknown, use QUOTE_UPDATE_AGE_UNKNOWN, not "fresh age=0."
Source metadata or a verified adapter must establish which clock is provided.

Raw archival inclusion does not require an arbitrary liquidity filter. The primary
causal numerical view uses same completed-minute source records where available;
older values remain archived and appear in a separately labeled as-of view with
explicit age. Any maximum-age filter is a versioned analysis setting, never a
silent new source-data rule. Sensitivity results must disclose changed populations.

7.5 Features and publication times

Filter observations by availability BEFORE aggregation or feature construction.
Rolling statistics use only already revealed bars. No centered rolling window,
future interpolation, full-day volume total, or full-sample normalization is allowed.
Use only oi_before for intraday OI. Same-session oi is post-session evidence only,
as mandated by SOURCE-OPTIONS. An event's later actual value or revised history
cannot be used in a pre-event feature.

7.6 Trading sessions versus elapsed time

Option observations and hypothetical actions require eligible sessions. Underlying
risk may evolve while options are closed. Store overnight and weekend gaps as
calendar-duration intervals; do not compress them into a one-minute diffusion step.
On early closes, do not fabricate normal-session minutes. At the final regular
bar's availability boundary, do not assume an immediate regular-session fill exists.

Use epoch differences for tau across DST changes. Date-DTE remains a NY date gap.
A lifecycle spanning a DST transition must not silently lose or gain an hour in
its pricing clock through naive datetime subtraction.

==============================================================================
08. QUALITY STATES, MISSINGNESS, AND COVERAGE DENOMINATORS
==============================================================================

Raw validation follows the two canonical contracts. Research-specific filters
operate on derived views and never delete the raw source or contract inventory.

Minimum observation states:
  VALID_TWO_SIDED; VALID_ZERO_BID; LOCKED_MARKET; CROSSED_MARKET;
  INVALID_NUMERIC; STALE_ASOF; QUOTE_UPDATE_AGE_UNKNOWN; NO_OBSERVATION;
  LISTED_BUT_UNQUOTED; NOT_YET_LISTED_VERIFIED; LISTING_STATUS_UNKNOWN;
  UNDERLYING_MISSING; TIMING_AMBIGUOUS; IV_NOT_IDENTIFIABLE;
  TERMINAL_REFERENCE_MISSING; UNSUPPORTED_DELIVERABLE.

Canonical validity includes finite bid/ask, bid >= 0, ask > 0, and ask >= bid.
A zero bid can be valid. It is not a guaranteed positive liquidation value.
An ask of zero is not a valid two-sided quote under SOURCE-OPTIONS.
A midpoint is defined only for an accepted quote and is not an executable fill.
Quote size absent from the schema is UNKNOWN, not zero and not unlimited liquidity.

Coverage must be reported at these separate levels:
  - Expiration calendar coverage: verified expected expirations versus discovered.
  - Session-expiration partition coverage: expected versus present/validated files.
  - Contract coverage: verified listed versus observed, when denominator is known.
  - Contract-time coverage: observed valid/missing/stale states on the study grid.
  - IV/model coverage: observations for which specific transformations are supported.
  - Terminal coverage: verified endpoint/payoff evidence versus incomplete lifecycles.

Example denominator rule:
  quote_coverage = valid_quote_slots / expected_slots_for_verified_listed_contracts.
If listing intervals are unknown, that metric is unavailable. Instead report
coverage relative to the retrospective observed universe and label it explicitly.
Do not count pre-listing slots as missing for a verified later-listed contract.

Do not retain only complete lifecycles. Missingness can depend on price, liquidity,
market stress, or strike location. The default is to preserve masks and estimate
observable-conditional quantities with coverage warnings. A complete-case statistic
must say "conditional on the required observations being available."

Do not claim that generic imputation, survival analysis, or inverse-probability
weights eliminate selection bias. Such corrections require explicit assumptions
about the missingness process and their own sensitivity analysis.

No future quote backfill. No replacement of a missing terminal quote with zero.
No model-produced observation may enter a table labeled actual historical data.
Keep observed, contemporaneously interpolated, forecast, and stressed values in
separate fields with origin tags.

Every cohort remains in coverage reports, including ineligible, sparse, corrupt,
unfinished, and unverified-endpoint cases. Model-specific support exclusions must
be reported by reason, period, right, initial/current moneyness, and DTE phase.

==============================================================================
09. SAMPLING, WEIGHTING, AND DEPENDENCE
==============================================================================

Archive the full available one-minute panel. Do not equate row count with the number
of independent market experiences. All strikes in a chain share market movements;
neighboring expirations share calendar events and often overlapping lifecycle days.

Default reporting estimands:
  A. Expiration-balanced contract lifecycle outcomes:
     compute a statistic within each expiration and initial-moneyness/right cell,
     then average the expiration statistics equally among contributing cohorts.
  B. Chain dynamics at standardized lifecycle phases:
     each chain snapshot is one observation; normalize weights within expiration
     and phase so longer sessions or denser chains do not dominate by row count.
  C. Calendar-time market behavior:
     one decision time receives a declared weight across simultaneously observed
     expirations, rather than multiplying its influence by the number of series.

For a cell B, a basic descriptive mean can be written:
  mean_B = (1 / N_E(B)) * sum_E [ sum_i w_Ei * y_Ei / sum_i w_Ei ],
where i indexes eligible observations in that expiration/cell, w_Ei is a declared
within-expiration weight, and N_E(B) counts contributing expirations.
Report both N_E and the underlying contract, decision-time, and calendar-day counts.

Volume-weighted or OI-weighted summaries are OPTIONAL alternative estimands.
Only historically known OI is eligible. A volume-weighted view must not replace
the all-strike view or turn illiquid contracts into nonexistent contracts.

Model-training anchor grid:
  Default: one chain anchor every five completed regular-session minutes, plus the
  first and final supported completed-bar anchors. Preserve one-minute raw outcomes.
  Rationale: reduce near-duplicate training examples, not change the observed market.
  Run sensitivity at one- and fifteen-minute anchors. A training anchor choice is
  distinct from the outcome monitoring frequency.

Use calendar-time block resampling for uncertainty. The block must carry all
contracts and expirations for its time span together. Expiration-cluster-only
resampling is insufficient when different expirations overlap in calendar time.
Block length is selected/justified from training dependence diagnostics and must
cover the longest outcome horizon being analyzed; evaluate longer alternatives.

Do not manufacture an exact independent effective sample size. A kernel-weight
ESS = 1/sum(normalized_weight^2) is only a weight-concentration diagnostic and must
not be advertised as the count of independent market observations.

==============================================================================
10. COORDINATES, ECONOMIC FEATURES, AND UNIT CONVENTIONS
==============================================================================

Let c be an exact contract, r its right C/P, K its strike, E its expiry, t the
actual decision time, S_t the authoritative revealed QQQ price, and M_c its multiplier.

Primary model-independent coordinates:
  x_s(c,t) = log(K / S_t)                 [spot log-moneyness]
  x_s0(c)  = log(K / S_t0)                [frozen initial coordinate, when known]
  p_norm(c,t) = option_price(c,t) / S_t   [dimensionless price]

Conditional forward coordinate:
  k(c,t) = log(K / F_tE), only when F_tE is supported by point-in-time inputs.
  k0(c)  = log(K / F_t0E), frozen at entry when supported.

Do not silently substitute x_s for k inside a trained model. Store coordinate_type
and train/evaluate separate schemas when forward inputs are unavailable.

A declared deterministic-carry approximation may use:
  D(t,u) = discount factor from the as-of rate curve;
  PV_div(t,E) = sum_j [D(t,d_j) * announced_expected_cash_dividend_j];
  F_tE = (S_t - PV_div(t,E)) / D(t,T_E).
This formula requires the declared financing/carry assumptions and suitable dividend
inputs. Continuous-yield alternative: F_tE = S_t*exp((r-q)*tau). Never invent r, q,
or a zero dividend. Do not use European put-call parity on raw American prices to
pretend the forward is uniquely observed. Unknown carry -> forward feature missing.

Intrinsic value for a standard deliverable:
  I_C(S,K) = max(S-K, 0)
  I_P(S,K) = max(K-S, 0)
  time_value_mid = midpoint - I_r(S,K)

Keep negative observed midpoint-minus-intrinsic values as diagnostics. They can
reflect timing, quote, or model incompatibility; do not erase them by clipping raw
prices. A separately fitted economically consistent price has a different origin.

Liquidity features:
  spread_abs = ask - bid
  midpoint = (bid + ask)/2
  spread_relative = spread_abs / midpoint, only when midpoint > 0
  spread_spot = spread_abs / S_t
  zero_bid flag; quote age and its basis; source coverage; quote sizes if present;
  observed trade count/volume up to t; known prior OI; missingness indicators.

Underlying features, all causal:
  log returns over 1, 5, and 30 observed eligible minute intervals;
  rolling realized variance = sum_j (log(S_j/S_{j-1}))^2 over declared windows;
  overnight return as a distinct feature; revealed intraday range; distance from
  session open; missing/warm-up flags; actual elapsed durations of each interval.
Do not compare a raw 30-minute variance directly to annualized IV squared. For a
variance-risk comparison, align forecast horizon, clock, and annualization basis.

Clock/context features:
  actual tau; date-DTE; active minutes remaining; minute of session; weekday;
  early-close and overnight/weekend flags; known scheduled-event proximity;
  known ex-dividend proximity; listing-rule era; historical product rule version.
Unknown event information remains unknown. Do not label a past state by its future
realized volatility regime, future return, or eventual max-profit outcome.

Optional expected-move coordinate:
  z = log(K/F_tE) / sqrt(w_ATM), where w_ATM > 0 and is reliably identified.
Do not use z as the only coordinate; it becomes unstable as expiry/variance tends
to zero and depends on the chosen IV representation. Store validity flags.

Percent option returns are secondary. Near-zero premium denominators can dominate
them. Prefer dollar changes, spot-scaled price changes, and explicitly defined
capital/risk-normalized outcomes. Do not apply an arbitrary premium floor and then
claim the statistic still describes every strike.

==============================================================================
11. AMERICAN PRICING, IMPLIED VOLATILITY, AND EXPIRY LIMITS
==============================================================================

Raw price research does not depend on successful IV inversion. Treat IV as a
model-dependent representation of a price, not as an independently observed fact.
Vendor IV and Greeks remain diagnostic inputs unless their pricing conventions are
verified. Preserve both vendor and recomputed fields with separate model identifiers.

QQQ is being studied as an ETF-option product. Standard ETF options are American
and physically delivered; adjusted contracts need separate metadata. This distinction
is supported by [PROV-1]. Historically effective contract rules still need verification.
Do not silently replace American pricing with European Black-Scholes pricing.

11.1 Pricing contract

For a supported standard contract, define an American pricing approximation by:
  A_r(S,K,t,T,sigma; rates, dividends, carry)
    = sup over eligible stopping times u in [t,T]
      E_Q[ D(t,u) * I_r(S_u,K) | S_t=S ].

This is a PRICING representation under Q, not the empirical forecasting law P.
The constant sigma in an IV inversion is a quoting convention, not a claim that
actual historical volatility is constant throughout the contract's life.

Recommended numerical implementation:
  - Exercise-aware finite-difference solver with a documented deterministic rate
    curve and either discrete cash dividends or a separately declared yield model.
  - Between dividend events, solve the obstacle problem:
      min( -V_t - L V, V-I_r ) = 0,
      L V = 0.5*sigma^2*S^2*V_SS + (r-q)*S*V_S - r*V.
  - At maturity: V(S,T) = I_r(S,K).
  - At a deterministic cash ex-dividend event d with payment amount Dcash:
      continuation_before(S) = V_after(max(S-Dcash,0));
      V_before(S) = max(I_r(S,K), continuation_before(S)).
    Record that this stock-jump convention is a model approximation; do not apply
    it to unsupported special dividends/deliverables.
  - Use an implicit startup around payoff/dividend nonsmoothness; a converged
    complementarity solver for the exercise constraint; refined space/time grids
    around the strike, dividends, and near expiry.
  - Expand domain and refine grids until price differences meet a configured USD
    tolerance materially smaller than the quote scale used in the research.
  - Check price monotonicity, intrinsic bounds, and nonnegative vega where expected.
  - Compare benchmark cases with an independently implemented exercise-aware tree
    or another independently validated solver. Solver agreement is numerical
    evidence, not proof that the pricing model matches the market.

Numerical boundaries must match the declared carry/dividend model and be tested
by domain expansion. Do not use an undocumented far-boundary shortcut to claim
universal accuracy. Save solver version, grids, tolerances, and convergence results.

11.2 IV identification from an interval, not only a midpoint

For bid b and ask a, define the compatible volatility set:
  IV_set = {sigma >= 0 : A_r(sigma) lies within [b,a], allowing declared numerical
            pricing tolerance}.

Use a monotone bracketed solver to find bounds where identifiable. Expand the
upper bracket only within a declared solver domain; an exhausted bracket is a
failure status, not a clipped IV. Flat exercise-value regions can produce a range
of compatible sigma values, not a uniquely determined number.

Report:
  iv_mid when a meaningful midpoint inversion exists;
  iv_low, iv_high, compatible_set_status;
  price_sensitivity_to_sigma (vega), numerical_pricing_error;
  model/input provenance; iv_identifiability flags.

Local diagnostic:
  approximate_iv_error = approximate_price_error / vega,
when vega is nonzero and the local linear approximation is appropriate.
A wide interval or very small vega means IV is weakly identified. Do not count a
large IV percentage jump as economic evidence without checking the price interval.

A supported IV-factor view needs a predeclared uncertainty tolerance. Select that
tolerance using training diagnostics and report sensitivity; do not filter the
raw whole-chain archive. If no tolerance is justified, publish intervals and use
price-space features rather than a false precise IV series.

11.3 Total variance and limiting behavior

For an identified IV and tau > 0:
  w(k,t,E) = iv(k,t,E)^2 * tau.
Total variance is a useful representation [PROV-2], but it does not automatically
remove measurement noise or impose correct American pricing behavior.

At tau=0, do not invert IV, divide by tau, or compute expected-move coordinates.
Use an explicitly labeled payoff boundary when a valid terminal reference exists.
The pre-expiry American option's value and an actual exercised account's cash/share
outcome are not interchangeable quantities.

Missing rates/dividends/product inputs:
  Raw price, spread, coverage, and spot-moneyness analyses may continue.
  Unsupported IV, forward, and model-Greek outputs remain null with a reason.
  Do not invent missing inputs to keep a model pipeline running.

==============================================================================
12. WHOLE-CHAIN PRICE FITTING AND ECONOMIC CONSISTENCY
==============================================================================

Price-space fitting is the primary reconstruction layer. IV-space fitting is a
secondary supported view. Preserve raw quotes; never overwrite them with a fit.
Use contemporaneous information only, and fit calls and puts without forcing raw
American prices into European put-call parity.

12.1 Inputs and objective

For one expiration/right/decision time, order supported strikes K_1 < ... < K_n.
Let p_i be fitted prices, [b_i,a_i] the observed quote intervals, m_i their midpoints,
and u_i a declared positive measurement scale based on bid/ask width and verified
price resolution. A numerical floor is allowed but must not be misrepresented as
an exchange tick size. Do not assume every contract has the same tick convention.

Define distance to an interval:
  dist(p,[b,a]) = max(b-p, 0, p-a).

Fit a constrained curve with an objective such as:
  sum_i alpha_i * (dist(p_i,[b_i,a_i]) / u_i)^2
  + lambda_mid * sum_i alpha_i * ((p_i-m_i)/u_i)^2
  + lambda_smooth * ||D2_scaled p||^2.

D2_scaled is a second divided-difference operator respecting irregular strike
spacing. Work in normalized price/strike units for numerical conditioning, while
reporting all final reconstruction errors in actual option-price and USD units.

alpha_i weights must be capped to prevent one artificially narrow/stale quote from
determining the entire chain. Do not give a noisy zero-premium wing observation an
infinite weight. Record the influence function/weights for audit.

Default model selection:
  Include a constrained piecewise-linear baseline.
  Compare smoothing penalties using masked-strike reconstruction tests within
  TRAINING snapshots; then evaluate on held-out dates and strike regions.
  Choose the least complex fit satisfying the declared errors and support rules.
  Do not choose smoothness by how profitable its later backtest appears.

12.2 Explicit cross-strike constraints

For standard contracts with identical expiry, deliverable, and underlying:
  Calls: p_{i+1} <= p_i.
  Puts:  p_{i+1} >= p_i.
  Both: (p_{i+1}-p_i)/(K_{i+1}-K_i)
          <= (p_{i+2}-p_{i+1})/(K_{i+2}-K_{i+1}).
  Both: p_i >= immediate_exercise_intrinsic_i.

Under the explicitly supported deterministic-discount setup, let
  B(t,T) = max_{u in [t,T]} D(t,u), including D(t,t)=1.
Then optional strike-slope bounds are [-B,0] for calls and [0,B] for puts.
Do not replace B with 1 when that is incompatible with the rate assumptions.
A put upper bound K*B and a call upper bound S require their stated standard
product/carry assumptions. Unknown inputs disable those dependent bounds.

These are useful consistency constraints, NOT a complete proof that a fitted
American chain and its subsequent dynamics admit no arbitrage. Never transfer a
European SVI or density-extraction theorem to American data without its conditions.
[PROV-2] supports the importance of constrained surface construction, not that
these American research fits inherit all guarantees of that European framework.

Do not enforce decreasing option prices or decreasing IV along a historical
lifecycle. Those observations occur under different market conditions. Time-series
aging is not the same mathematical comparison as simultaneous cross-expiry prices.

12.3 Inconsistent observations

Fit within intervals when feasible. If constraints and intervals conflict, retain
explicit slacks and diagnostics. Save which quotes required adjustment and by how
much; compare conflict magnitude with spread/timing uncertainty. A large conflict
can make the snapshot unsupported for modeling. It is not automatically a tradable
arbitrage opportunity, and must not be repaired invisibly.

12.4 Interpolation versus extrapolation

Every fitted value carries one of:
  OBSERVED_QUOTE; WITHIN_SUPPORTED_INTERPOLATION; MODEL_EXTRAPOLATION;
  FORECAST_RECONSTRUCTION; UNSUPPORTED.

Interpolation is permitted only within a declared support region, with errors
measured on withheld observed strikes. A large empty strike interval requires a
support flag even if it lies inside the outermost observed strikes. Do not infer
a well-measured wing from two distant endpoints.

No production inference outside validated support. All actual strikes still appear
in raw descriptive outputs, including those outside the learned representation.
No unobserved strike is presented as historically listed merely because the curve
can be evaluated there.

==============================================================================
13. INTERPRETABLE REPRESENTATION OF THE WHOLE CHAIN
==============================================================================

Goal: compress shared structure enough to understand and forecast it WITHOUT
pretending that a few factors exactly characterize every strike or payoff.

13.1 Primary representation

For supported regions, define normalized time value:
  y_r(x,t,E) = (fitted_price_r(K,t,E) - I_r(S_t,K)) / S_t.

Use a masked, regularized functional factor representation:
  y_r(x,t,E)
    = mean_r(x, tau, time_of_day; training_parameters)
      + sum_{j=1..J} z_j(t,E) * phi_{r,j}(x,tau)
      + residual_r(x,t,E).

The basis functions phi and mean are learned ONLY from the training partition.
Use irregular-grid/masked observations or a supported interpolated grid with an
explicit mask; do not turn unobserved wings into observed zero values.
The same z vector should capture joint call/put structure when supported; retain
right-specific residuals rather than forcing identical call/put IV behavior.

Initial implementation:
  - Build a training-defined spline/grid basis over observed spot-moneyness and
    actual tau support. Include near-expiry resolution and broad observed wings.
  - Fit a regularized masked low-rank representation of chain shapes.
  - Compare J in a small declared candidate set, e.g. 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12.
  - Select J using held-out reconstruction AND forecast quality, not explained
    variance alone. High overall variance explained can hide poor wing behavior.
  - Publish per-strike-region and per-phase residual errors.

Those J values are engineering candidates, not claims about the true factor count.
A price-space decoder must recheck Section 12 constraints on the actual contracts.
If projection is needed, record its size and effect on uncertainty and forecasts.
Do not use large projections to conceal a badly specified factor model.

13.2 Interpretability layer

Show direct, named shape measurements alongside abstract factors:
  supported ATM price/IV interval; downside/upside wing price contrasts;
  skew and curvature over explicitly stated coordinates; near-ATM slope;
  call/put residual differences; spread and quote-availability profiles.

A factor may be labeled "level-like" or "skew-like" only after inspecting its
training loadings and stability. PCA axes can rotate or change sign between fits.
Align factor presentation using training-only references, not future data.

13.3 Optional IV representation

Where IV is identified, fit:
  w_r(k,t,E) = f_r(k,tau; Z_tE) + residual_r(k,t,E).

This is an alternative diagnostic coordinate system, not permission to drop raw
prices or to imply that one observed maturity contains a full term structure.
A maturity term structure requires multiple expirations at the SAME time. Use
neighboring expirations only as explicitly sourced contemporaneous context.

13.4 Fixed-contract repricing and moving coordinates

For a fixed contract K, recompute its current coordinate from the forecast spot or
forward at every step. Do not hold a moneyness coordinate fixed and describe the
result as the same contract. The mapping back to actual K and multiplier is required
before calculating any P&L, spread value, or first-hit event.

13.5 Local Greek attribution, diagnostic only

When a consistent pricing model and IV are supported, compare observed changes with:
  delta_price_approx = Delta_t * delta_S
                    + Theta_t * delta_t
                    + 0.5 * Gamma_t * delta_S^2
                    + Vega_t * delta_sigma
                    + Vanna_t * delta_S * delta_sigma
                    + 0.5 * Vomma_t * delta_sigma^2.

All sensitivities must share the declared units and model; vendor theta-per-day
cannot be multiplied by years. Residual = observed_change - approximation, and may
include model error, jumps, rates/dividends, higher orders, and quote noise.
Do not treat this local approximation as a reliable path model near expiry or as
causal proof. A repricing-based attribution depends on the order of counterfactual
changes; disclose that order or a separately implemented symmetric attribution.

==============================================================================
14. OUTCOME DEFINITIONS AND LABEL GENERATION
==============================================================================

No model is trained until labels are independently tested. Feature generation and
future-label generation must be separate modules and output namespaces.

14.1 Registered horizons

Primary direct horizons:
  1, 5, 15, 30, and 60 REGULAR-SESSION minutes, within the same session by default.

Additional named horizons:
  current_session_final_observation;
  next_session_first_scheduled_completed_bar;
  verified_expiry_payoff_boundary.

The regular-session minute horizon does not automatically jump across a close.
If insufficient same-session minutes remain, label HORIZON_OUTSIDE_SESSION rather
than silently changing the duration. A separate cross-session clock profile may
be studied, recording its actual calendar duration and intervening closure.

Named endpoints are scheduled in advance. Never move an endpoint to the next
convenient valid quote. A missing observation at the requested endpoint stays
missing. Terminal intrinsic and final observed option quote are separate targets.

14.2 Contract-level outcomes

For contract c observed at t and requested endpoint t_h:
  delta_mid_c = mid_c(t_h) - mid_c(t)
  delta_normalized_price_c = delta_mid_c / S_t
  delta_time_value_c = [mid_c(t_h)-I_r(S_t_h,K)] - [mid_c(t)-I_r(S_t,K)]
  delta_iv_c and delta_w_c only if both endpoints support the chosen representation.
  spread_change; zero_bid_transition; valid_quote_presence_at_t_h;
  future quote-age/coverage state, where observable.

Also calculate path extrema on the DECLARED sampled grid:
  max/min midpoint or executable-side mark change;
  maximum adverse/favorable excursion under a specified hypothetical side;
  sampled first-hit times for predeclared absolute or bounded-strategy targets.

Each outcome records its initial/current moneyness grouping, horizon, price basis,
coverage, contract identity, and units. Do not mix rolling-ATM and fixed-ID labels.

14.3 Whole-chain outcomes

For the same trained representation at both endpoints:
  Y_t,h = [log(S_t_h/S_t), Z_t_h-Z_t, liquidity_changes,
           quote_presence_changes, supported_shape_residual_summaries].

All endpoint shapes may use information actually available at that endpoint for
LABELS. Their basis/normalizer and fit hyperparameters remain training-frozen.
Future labels must never be joined back into current features accidentally.

14.4 Missing outcomes and first-hit bounds

For a sampled-grid hit event, classify each lifecycle/anchor as:
  SUCCESS_OBSERVED: an eligible observed sample proves the specified event.
  FAILURE_OBSERVED: all required eligible samples exist and none meets the event.
  UNRESOLVED: missing samples or boundary evidence prevent classification.

With H observed successes, F observed failures, and U unresolved cases, a simple
unweighted identification interval for the sampled event is:
  lower = H / (H+F+U)
  upper = (H+U) / (H+F+U).
Use matching weighted bounds for a weighted estimand. These are missing-data
identification bounds, NOT statistical confidence intervals.

Continuous-time hits are a different event: even a complete one-minute grid may
miss an intra-minute touch. Do not claim the interval above solves continuous-time
monitoring error. Higher-frequency evidence or an explicitly validated bridge
model would be a separate extension with its own assumptions.

Summing different legs' separate minute highs/lows is not a synchronized portfolio
price. A combo hit requires compatible timestamps and the declared execution basis.

14.5 Right-censored archive tails

An expiration whose lifecycle extends beyond the archive cutoff is unfinished,
not a completed failure. Maintain its observed prefix and censored status. Never
assign a zero return or a loss merely to populate a fixed-length tensor.

==============================================================================
15. EMPIRICAL JOINT DYNAMICS: MODELS TO IMPLEMENT FIRST
==============================================================================

The target is a conditional historical/physical distribution, not a pricing-law
identity:
  Law_P( delta_log_S, delta_Z, delta_L, future_observation_state | X_t, horizon ).

P denotes actual-market statistical forecasting. Q denotes a pricing measure.
A good fit to current option prices does not uniquely identify P or a trading edge;
see [PROV-4]. Do not reuse a risk-neutral/frozen-IV P50 simulator as historical truth.

15.1 State X_t

Include available current market/chain features from Sections 10 and 13, exact
remaining clocks, current support/missingness masks, and selected causal return/
volatility/liquidity history. Optional adjacent expiries require their own as-of
coverage. No feature may identify a future outcome class.

A compact state is an approximation to history, not a proven Markov-sufficient
summary. Test residual dependence and whether additional past state improves
chronological forecasts. Do not keep adding lags without validation.

15.2 Mandatory baseline B0: time/phase-conditioned empirical outcomes

Estimate empirical distributions within predeclared right/moneyness/time/phase
cells, with partial pooling when necessary. Keep the whole-chain response vector
together for joint targets. This measures the value of basic calendar structure.

Also include a no-change chain-shape baseline and a simple carry/aging benchmark
where pricing inputs permit one. Neither benchmark is assumed to be the real law.

15.3 Mandatory baseline B1: conditional historical analogs

Algorithm:
  1. Keep only training anchors whose labels were available by the model cutoff.
  2. Standardize features using training-only robust center/scale; remove constant
     features explicitly rather than dividing by zero.
  3. Compare candidates using a declared distance and eligible clock/event strata.
  4. Assign kernel weights, for example exp(-distance^2/(2*bandwidth^2)).
  5. Cap concentration by expiration/calendar block; normalize weights.
  6. Use the resulting weighted WHOLE response vectors or whole path segments as
     empirical joint scenarios. Never independently shuffle return, skew, and spread.
  7. Return weighted means, quantiles, event probabilities, support counts, analog
     distances, missing-label rates, and dependence-aware uncertainty.

Tune bandwidth, feature subset, and neighbor count only within development folds.
Initial engineering search: neighbor caps 50/100/200; bandwidth selected from
training-distance quantiles. These are not market facts or guarantees.

Missing dimensions require explicitly declared matching/target masks. A forecast
conditional on complete endpoints is not automatically the unconditional law.
If adequate joint observations are unavailable, provide a narrower supported
forecast or abstain; do not independently fill each wing and claim a joint sample.

15.4 Candidate B2: regularized conditional mean plus joint residuals

For each named horizon, fit a parsimonious multivariate model:
  Y_t,h = g_h(X_t) + epsilon_t,h.

Start with ridge-regularized linear/additive effects for clocks, chain factors,
and selected interactions. Estimate uncertainty using joint residual vectors or
calendar blocks, conditioned on supported variance regimes when justified.
Preserve return/shape/liquidity dependence and observed tail structure.

Do not use independent Gaussian noise per strike, or assume normally distributed
residuals merely for convenience. Compare empirical residual sampling with more
structured distributions only on held-out forecasts.

15.5 More complex models, conditional on evidence

Distributional nonlinear models, temporal networks, or stochastic differential
models are OPTIONAL candidates, not initial prerequisites. They must outperform
B0/B1/B2 on declared proper scores, calibration, tails, and stability, after model
selection costs and uncertainty. Research on joint option/underlying factor dynamics
is background precedent [PROV-3], not proof that a particular architecture works here.

A model can remain useful for DESCRIPTION even when it fails forecasting gates.
A forecasting model can pass statistical tests while still failing cost-adjusted
trade evaluation. Keep those statuses separate.

15.6 Chain-consistent reconstruction

For a forecast sample:
  reconstruct positive underlying price from its log return;
  advance actual maturity/time/calendar state;
  map each requested fixed strike to its new coordinate;
  decode the supported joint chain and retain residual uncertainty;
  verify price/exercise/payoff constraints and support;
  model liquidity/availability rather than treating every theoretical price as a fill.

Large or frequent constraint projections are a model failure diagnostic. Save
pre/post-projection distributions and check whether projection destroys observed
skew, tails, or portfolio risk. Static snapshot constraints alone do not establish
fully dynamically arbitrage-free American market dynamics.

==============================================================================
16. MULTI-STEP PATHS, OBSERVATION MODELS, AND BOUNDARIES
==============================================================================

Direct horizon distributions are sufficient for many descriptive and forecasting
questions. They are NOT automatically a mutually consistent stochastic process.
Do not multiply endpoint probabilities or independently sample each horizon to
obtain a path, first-hit probability, or adjustment-policy value.

Two permitted path methods:

PATH-A: historical analog continuation segments
  Select an eligible historical anchor using information available at the query.
  Keep its subsequent joint return/shape/liquidity/availability segment together.
  Align lifecycle/calendar phase under a declared mapping; preserve actual closure
  lengths. For a raw historical replay, retain the actual prices and identities.
  A reanchored synthetic scenario is a model-generated path and must be labeled as
  such, mapped back to fixed requested contracts, and independently validated.

PATH-B: learned transition process
  Learn one-step transitions plus separately specified overnight/closure and
  near-expiry transitions. Roll forward recursively using only simulated/current
  state, not the actual future state (no teacher forcing in evaluation).
  Validate multi-step distributions and first-hit behavior against real held-out
  lifecycles, even if one-step scores look good.

Use the simpler method with demonstrated support. Do not declare PATH-B necessary
before the descriptive atlas or direct-horizon forecasts can be delivered.

A quote observation model is needed when simulated scenarios include missing or
stale markets. Distinguish an unobserved latent price from an actual executable
quote. Do not let missing liquidity disappear in favorable simulated outcomes.

Expiry transition:
  Treat intrinsic payoff as a boundary for a standard supported option valuation,
  using a verified reference price and contract terms. Do not extrapolate an IV
  formula through tau=0. Stop or transform option exposure according to the chosen
  account/payoff convention; actual exercise creates share/cash obligations.

Overnight/closed sessions:
  Price risk can change; hypothetical option transactions cannot occur unless the
  product/session adapter explicitly supports them. Keep event and jump sensitivity
  separate from empirical probabilities when historical support is insufficient.

Rare events:
  Empirical samples cannot guarantee accurate probabilities for unobserved tails.
  Add explicitly labeled stress scenarios without blending their assumed weights
  into empirical probability estimates. No stress scenario is fabricated history.

==============================================================================
17. CHRONOLOGICAL SPLITS, PURGING, AND MODEL SELECTION
==============================================================================

Random row splitting is prohibited. The same expiration lifecycle cannot contribute
ordinary training examples and held-out test examples to the same evaluation fold.
Overlapping expirations and overlapping future labels require calendar-time controls.

17.1 Split manifest before exploratory model selection

Default engineering proposal, subject to adequate coverage:
  - Seal the final 20% of calendar-session coverage as the final chronological test.
  - Use the earlier 80% for expanding-window development/validation.
  - Within development, use the first 60% of its time span for initial training;
    validate on the next four consecutive 10% blocks, expanding training each time.
  - Determine splits from times/reference coverage, not from observed profitability.

These percentages are a starting protocol, not a statistical guarantee. Save the
actual calendar boundaries and cohort IDs before tuning. If this produces inadequate
counts, report insufficient predictive-validation support; do not silently consume
the sealed test or rename training fit as validation.

17.2 Lifecycle assignment and purge rules

Assign cohorts by entry date, then check their complete information/label intervals.
At each training cutoff, only use labels already available by that cutoff.
Remove training observations/cohorts whose future-label interval intersects the
held-out prediction period, or that would share the same lifecycle across partitions.
Use actual interval endpoints, including closures and terminal labels, not a fixed
assumption that four DTE always means four trading days.

A held-out feature may legitimately use historical observations preceding its
prediction time. Past feature lookback is not itself future leakage. Nevertheless,
report such overlap and consider a buffer sensitivity when serial dependence is
material. Do not implement "purging" by arbitrarily deleting all common history.

For overlapping expirations observed on the same dates, use chronological block
boundaries and purge boundary-straddling lifecycle labels. Preserve cross-expiry
co-movement in uncertainty calculations even after labels are correctly purged.

17.3 Fit everything within the fold

Fit scalers, bins chosen from data, bases, means, PCA/factor axes, imputers, state
clusters, feature selection, smoothing settings, density estimators, thresholds,
and hyperparameters on TRAINING data only. A basis learned from the entire archive
is leakage even if the final regression is trained on a past subset.

Training-only factor/basis artifacts must be used to encode validation states and
future label shapes. Current-snapshot fitting may use current quotes, but not a
future temporal smoothing window or future-selected hyperparameters.

17.4 Test-once discipline

Freeze data versions, code, selected models, hypotheses, and decision rules before
opening final test outcomes. If results lead to a redesign, label that test consumed.
A new confirmation needs a genuinely new later holdout or an explicitly nested
protocol, not another optimized rerun on the same test period.

Maintain an experiment registry of all meaningful variants, including failures.
Do not report only the best strike, horizon, regime, or algorithm after a broad
search. Backtest selection can overfit even with apparently sensible holdouts;
[PROV-5] supports keeping this a first-class research concern.

==============================================================================
18. VALIDATION METRICS AND RELEASE CRITERIA
==============================================================================

Report metrics by right, initial/current strike location, actual remaining time,
time of day, market era, volatility state, event proximity, liquidity, and coverage.
Do not allow aggregate averages to hide failures near expiry or in the wings.

18.1 Reconstruction metrics
  Absolute price error; error in USD per actual contract multiplier;
  spread-scaled error using a declared positive scale;
  fraction within observed bid/ask interval;
  constraint violation counts/slacks;
  errors on withheld strikes and withheld wing regions;
  residual error by coverage/quote-age class;
  measured domain/interpolation/extrapolation coverage.

Midpoints are a noisy benchmark, not exact economic truth. Compare results under
bid/ask-compatible target perturbations and disclose sensitivity. Never validate a
surface solely against another smooth surface fitted by the same method.

18.2 Forecast metrics

Mean forecasts: MAE/RMSE and signed bias, alongside relevant unconditional baselines.
Quantiles: pinball loss, empirical coverage, interval width, and crossing checks.
Probabilities: Brier score and calibration by probability bins/regime for clearly
identified sampled events.
Distributions: univariate CRPS and multivariate energy/variogram diagnostics.

For univariate scenario samples y_1,...,y_N and observation y:
  CRPS_hat = mean_i |y_i-y| - 0.5*mean_{i,j} |y_i-y_j|.
For a multivariate vector, replace absolute value with a norm to obtain an energy
score. Standardize dimensions with TRAINING scales; otherwise a large-unit variable
can dominate. Specify finite-sample estimator convention and keep it fixed.

A multivariate score alone is not enough: separately check tail dependence,
return/skew/spread relationships, and actual fixed-leg/spread outcome distributions.
A distribution with correct marginals can still have wrong portfolio dependence.

18.3 Path metrics, when paths are implemented
  Multi-step return/shape distributions; sampled first-hit curves;
  maximum adverse/favorable excursion distributions;
  conditional spread and availability behavior in adverse paths;
  overnight/weekend gap behavior; near-expiry transitions;
  absence of illegal transactions in closed periods;
  reconstruction/projection frequency and magnitude through a full lifecycle.

18.4 Numerical versus statistical versus model uncertainty

Keep these separate:
  NUMERICAL: solver/interpolation/discretization error.
  MEASUREMENT: bid/ask interval, timestamps, quotes, missing data.
  SAMPLING: limited dependent historical observations.
  MODEL: representation, state, functional form, regime change, tail assumptions.
  EXECUTION: actual fills, latency, size, assignment, liquidation costs.

Do not add unrelated uncertainties into a single unjustified "95% confidence"
number. Label block-bootstrap confidence intervals, missingness bounds, prediction
intervals, and stress ranges by their actual meaning.

18.5 Practical release gates

Hard correctness gates:
  zero accepted known look-ahead violations;
  zero silently overwritten source records;
  zero unresolved identity/unit/time ambiguities entering affected computations;
  zero synthetic observations labeled actual;
  complete exclusion/coverage accounting and reproducible splits.

Prediction gates:
  comparisons on identical supported evaluation cases;
  paired calendar-block uncertainty for score differences;
  no release claim of improvement when differences are unresolved;
  tail/near-expiry and calibration diagnostics pass predeclared tolerances;
  support counts and missingness do not contradict the claimed scope.

Suggested initial evidence floors for a "supported forecast" label:
  at least 30 distinct contributing expirations and 20 calendar blocks locally;
  adequate independent development and final-test cohorts for the registered claim.
These are conservative engineering warning thresholds, NOT sufficient conditions
for accuracy, independence, significance, or reliable tail probabilities. Thin
regions may remain descriptive-only even above these counts. Final numerical
error/coverage tolerances must be frozen before evaluating sealed test results.

==============================================================================
19. IDENTIFICATION, MULTIPLE TESTING, AND NONSTATIONARITY
==============================================================================

19.1 Aging is not an isolated experiment

Within one expiration, remaining time and calendar time move together. A price
change along that path cannot automatically be attributed to "one less DTE."
Control/stratify for weekday, session time, contemporaneous market movement,
volatility/liquidity state, known events, and listing-rule era.

Use multiple expiration lifecycles and, where available, contemporaneous neighboring
expirations as comparison information. Do not claim a causal age effect without a
separately defensible identification design. The default output is conditional
association and prediction, not causality.

19.2 Listing and market structure change

Historical strike density, available expiration schedules, and data capture can
change over time. Track effective-dated metadata and period coverage. Do not apply
today's expiration calendar or a present-day strike universe retrospectively.
Changes in quote availability may be vendor/capture effects rather than economics.

19.3 Hypothesis management

Each tested relationship gets an experiment ID, prespecified population, metric,
horizon, comparison, and multiplicity family. A heatmap with hundreds of cells is
not hundreds of independent discoveries.

For confirmatory families, use a declared multiplicity procedure such as Holm
family-wise adjustment applied to valid dependence-aware tests. False-discovery
methods require their dependence assumptions to be justified, not assumed. Also
publish effect sizes, uncertainty, and out-of-sample replication; a p-value is not
an EV estimate or a guarantee of practical relevance.

Do not compute inferential p-values from independent-row standard errors when
observations share chains, lifecycles, or market shocks. Block methods also rely on
assumptions; show sensitivity to block length and structural regime changes.

19.4 Drift and unsupported states

Monitor feature/support shifts and residual calibration on genuinely later data.
A state outside training support returns OUTSIDE_VALIDATED_DOMAIN, not a confident
extrapolated forecast. Recency weighting, rolling windows, or regime-specific fits
are optional registered models requiring their own validation.

A model update receives a new version, training cutoff, and validation record.
Do not edit past predictions using later-fitted parameters and call the result a
real-time historical track record.

==============================================================================
20. INTERPRETABLE OUTPUTS: THE LIFECYCLE ATLAS
==============================================================================

The core user-facing deliverable is a lifecycle atlas backed by machine-readable
tables. It is not a screen of unexplained model scores or a promise of profit.

Required views:

A. Coverage and eligibility
  Expiration calendar matrix; expected/present sessions; listed versus observed
  universe evidence; missing/stale/zero-bid proportions; terminal coverage; exact
  4-calendar-DTE exclusions; number of contracts, expirations, dates, and blocks.

B. Fixed-contract lifecycle map
  Horizontal coordinate: elapsed lifecycle or remaining actual time.
  Vertical coordinate: frozen INITIAL moneyness, with calls and puts separated.
  Values: price/time-value changes and quantiles, dollar outcomes, spread behavior,
  quote availability, and supported IV changes. Show uncertainty and missingness.

C. Moving chain map
  Current moneyness versus remaining time/decision time; observed price shape,
  bid/ask envelopes, fitted support, residuals, and changed contract availability.
  Make clear that a moving-coordinate point is not necessarily one held contract.

D. Joint behavior and attribution
  Factor/loadings or direct shape-contrast views; joint underlying/wing/spread
  changes; regime-conditioned outcome distributions; diagnostic Greek attribution
  only where supported. Show relationships, not independent strike forecasts only.

E. Conditional forecast view
  As-of time, current supported state, requested horizon, empirical analog support,
  predicted mean/median/quantiles and uncertainty, forecast domain, and baseline
  comparisons. Distinguish historical description from a genuinely unseen forecast.

F. Model evaluation view
  Chronological fold performance, calibration, region/tail failures, uncertainty,
  missing-label rates, tested variants, and final-test status.

Each displayed result MUST expose:
  metric definition and units; population/cohort rule; start/end clock semantics;
  observed versus fitted/forecast/stressed origin; sample/coverage counts;
  weighting and uncertainty method; price/execution basis; run/model/data version;
  unsupported-region warnings and source-lineage references.

Do not smooth empty heatmap cells into apparent evidence. Mark empty/unsupported
cells explicitly. Display distributions/quantiles rather than only averages.
No populated chart or numerical probability is part of this specification: those
must be computed from the actual validated data during implementation.

==============================================================================
21. OPTIONAL DECISION LAYER: EXECUTION-AWARE EV, THEN P50
==============================================================================

Implement this only after descriptive and predictive gates pass. Studying every
strike does not mean searching every conceivable portfolio or future action tree.
A later policy engine must declare its actual action universe and search coverage.

21.1 First evaluate fixed, explicit actions

For a fixed signed position q_c in each contract and a specified entry/exit rule:
  Pi_net = sum_c [M_c*q_c*(exit_price_c - entry_price_c)]
           - commissions - fees - slippage_costs + other_declared_cashflows.

Quoted-side benchmark conventions:
  q_c > 0: enter at ask, exit at bid.
  q_c < 0: enter at bid, exit at ask.

If prices already include slippage, do not subtract it a second time. Apply actual
multipliers and quantities. Maintain financing/dividend/stock cashflows explicitly
when the supported exposure needs them. A midpoint result must be labeled theoretical.
A bid/ask-side result is still a quoted-price execution assumption, not proof of fill.

Decision and fill ordering:
  Compute the signal from information available at t.
  Use an execution model at t plus declared latency, or label a zero-latency quote
  benchmark. Do not use the same completed-bar low/high as a guaranteed fill.
  No fill while the relevant market is closed. No fill at an unobserved future quote.

Available quote sizes do not establish queue priority or multi-leg fill certainty.
With sizes absent, report a limited one-contract quoted benchmark and unsupported
capacity; do not scale freely to large size. A multi-leg order needs an explicitly
assumed synchronized combo model or conservative leg-side benchmark with legging
risk. Do not construct its fill from asynchronous favorable leg extremes.

21.2 Campaign accounting for adjustments

Maintain an immutable ledger of all signed execution cashflows, fees, stock changes,
financing, and dividends. Open option credits are NOT realized trading profits while
liabilities remain. At a liquidation decision:
  campaign_liquidation_PnL = sum(all executed net cashflows)
                            + current liquidation value of all remaining exposures
                            - prospective liquidation costs.

A "realized target" event requires the declared closing/realization action and all
associated liabilities/costs, not merely a large cash balance from selling options.
Keep mark-touch, quoted-close, simulated-filled-close, and actual-filled-close
statuses separate. Historical study data cannot establish actual user fills.

21.3 EV objective and P50 secondary objective

For a specified supported policy pi and finite campaign horizon T_hard:
  EV(pi | X_t) = E_P[total net campaign PnL under pi | X_t].

If and only if the opening structure has a finite positive original maximum profit:
  H0 = 0.50 * original_max_profit;
  P50_policy(pi) = P_P(campaign realizes at least H0 by T_hard under pi).

The original H0 is fixed through adjustments. Rolls do not reset losses, costs,
targets, or the hard horizon. A long call with unbounded theoretical maximum profit
has no finite "50% of max profit" target; show NOT_APPLICABLE unless a different,
explicit dollar target is chosen.

Possible later objective:
  lexicographically maximize ( EV(pi), P50_policy(pi) ) over feasible policies.

Strict lexicographic optimization uses P50 only to break genuine EV ties. Numerical
uncertainty is not proof of an economic tie: show statistically/numerically
indistinguishable candidates and uncertainty. An economic epsilon-EV tolerance is
an explicit user/model decision, not a silent sacrifice of EV for a higher P50.

Do not automatically close at 50% if the declared top priority is EV. A 50%-exit
policy is one candidate policy whose EV must be evaluated, not a universal rule.
Include NO_TRADE with incremental EV=0 as a baseline for new-entry decisions.

21.4 Constraints and study-support limits

Optional campaign defaults carried from the prior strategy discussion:
  buying-power cap = USD 5,000;
  cumulative-loss cap = USD 1,000;
  finite declared hard horizon; explicit permitted roll/extension rules.

These are decision-layer settings, not filters on the research archive. A stop-loss
order is not a guaranteed hard loss bound. Verified structural payoff bounds,
assignment exposure, realized losses, broker margin, and execution costs must be
accounted for before claiming that a strategy meets a hard cap.

A model trained only on 4-DTE-to-expiry chains does not automatically support a roll
into an unobserved 7-DTE contract or every cross-expiration portfolio. Require the
additional domains, contemporaneous quotes, metadata, and out-of-sample validation.
Return UNSUPPORTED_POLICY_DOMAIN rather than extrapolating universal management.

Standard ETF exercise/assignment may create shares [PROV-1]. Terminal intrinsic
value is not a complete brokerage cash/margin simulation. Use a separate account
adapter or keep account-level expiry results unsupported. Do not copy an index
cash-settlement convention into QQQ.

24. STORAGE, PERFORMANCE, AND REPRODUCIBILITY
==============================================================================

Reference desktop from the project context: Windows, i7-12700F, 32 GiB RAM, SSD/NVMe;
RTX 3080 may be available. Confirm actual runtime resources. GPU is optional and
must not be needed to run the core audit, panel, atlas, or baseline models.

Do not inherit the previous P50 engine's no-heavy-query architecture as a constraint
on this separate offline research project. Heavy offline fitting is acceptable;
it still needs measured resource use, reproducibility, and restartability.

Read and storage strategy:
  - Index filenames/metadata before loading values.
  - Read only needed source partitions and columns.
  - raw_theta gzip is sequential; row filtering does not avoid decompression.
  - Create optional normalized Parquet derivatives under research_root, not by
    overwriting immutable raw .csv.gz files.
  - Partition derivatives by semantic version, session/expiration, and study scope.
    Keep shared underlying observations separate rather than duplicating the full
    underlying archive for every expiration.
  - Process one or a bounded batch of session-expiration units at a time.
  - Represent absent slots using sparse observations plus expected-grid/mask logic
    where possible; do not allocate a huge dense all-contract Cartesian tensor.
  - Reuse validated per-snapshot pricing/shape computations across all strikes;
    do not reread the same gzip for each individual contract.

Initial resource limits are in Section 22. Measure per-task peak memory before
raising worker counts. Enforce the total memory budget across ALL workers and
library caches. Prevent nested BLAS/process oversubscription. Benchmark at 1/2/4/6
workers and record throughput and peak RAM; do not promise an unmeasured speedup.

An acquisition adapter must enforce a single overall provider concurrency limit.
The historical global/day settings in SOURCE-OPTIONS are not multiplied together
into a larger undocumented number of simultaneous requests.

Cache identity:
  source hashes + source-contract hashes + schema/clock interpretation + calendar
  version + cohort definition + feature/label specification + code version.
Learned caches also include fold_id, training cutoff, and training sample identity.
Invalidated caches are rebuilt as new artifacts, never relabeled as unchanged.

Reproducibility:
  stable contract sorting and row ordering;
  seeded sampling based on stable hashes of run/fold/task IDs, not process order;
  float64 numerical arrays unless a lower-precision implementation is separately
  shown to preserve declared tolerances;
  exact monetary/strike representation at accounting interfaces;
  deterministic or explicitly bounded parallel numerical differences;
  saved package/environment versions and solver settings;
  checkpointed tasks and atomic temp-file-to-final writes;
  append-only logs recording errors, exclusion counts, hashes, timings, and memory.

Do not truncate tails, remove strikes, silently reduce monitoring frequency, or
use an inappropriate European approximation merely to make the research faster.
Any performance approximation changes the model specification and needs validation.

26. END-TO-END ORCHESTRATION PSEUDOCODE
==============================================================================

The functions below describe implementation behavior, not runnable code supplied
with this document. Use explicit stage outputs and immutable artifact identities.

AUDIT AND DATA BUILD:

  contracts = read_required_source_contracts_exactly()
  assert_contracts_present_and_hash_them(contracts)
  config = validate_and_freeze_config(request, contracts)
  catalog = index_real_source_files(config)
  audit = validate_catalog_and_available_schemas(catalog)
  rules = resolve_versioned_calendar_clock_and_product_evidence(config)
  inventory = build_expiration_and_contract_inventory(catalog, available_metadata)
  cohorts = create_all_candidate_cohort_records(inventory, rules, config)
  gaps = compare_expected_and_observed_coverage(cohorts, catalog, inventory)
  save_atomic(audit, cohorts, gaps, provenance_manifest)

  for cohort in scheduled_supported_cohorts:
      units = required_session_expiration_units(cohort)
      for unit in units:
          rows = load_and_normalize_under_contracts(unit)
          observations = validate_preserve_flags_and_availability(rows, rules)
          snapshots = build_causal_completed_bar_snapshots(observations, rules)
          snapshots = join_authoritative_revealed_underlying(snapshots)
          snapshots = join_only_available_context_and_prior_OI(snapshots)
          persist_identity_preserving_panel_with_masks(snapshots)
      persist_lifecycle_boundary_and_coverage_status(cohort)

DESCRIPTION:

  estimands = freeze_descriptive_estimand_registry(config)
  raw_metrics = compute_price_liquidity_and_coverage_descriptions(panel, estimands)
  diagnostic_shapes = fit_declared_contemporaneous_descriptive_shapes(panel)
  publish_observed_vs_fitted_origins_and_support(raw_metrics, diagnostic_shapes)

  # A full-sample DESCRIPTIVE representation is allowed only if clearly labeled
  # retrospective. It must never be reused as a training-frozen forecast feature.

FORECAST DEVELOPMENT:

  splits = freeze_chronological_split_manifest(cohorts, actual_label_intervals)
  for fold in development_folds(splits):
      train, validation = enforce_cohort_and_actual_interval_purging(fold)
      pack = fit_all_learned_representation_parts(train_only=train)
      X_train = encode_causal_states(train, pack)
      Y_train = generate_future_labels(train, pack, label_spec)
      assert(all_labels_available_before_training_cutoff(Y_train))
      candidates = fit_registered_B0_B1_B2_models(X_train, Y_train)
      predictions = predict_validation_without_future_features(candidates, pack)
      outcomes = generate_validation_labels_separately(validation, pack)
      scores = paired_block_aware_evaluation(predictions, outcomes)
      save_all_candidates_scores_support_failures_and_artifacts(scores)

  selected = choose_registered_candidate_using_development_only()
  freeze_code_data_config_model_targets_and_tolerances(selected)
  final_pack = fit_selected_spec_on_eligible_pretest_data_only()
  final_predictions = predict_sealed_test_asof(final_pack)
  final_labels = reveal_and_evaluate_sealed_test_once(final_predictions)
  report_prediction_release_status(final_labels, support, uncertainty)

OPTIONAL ACTION EVALUATION:

  require_prediction_and_path_gates_for_requested_action_type()
  require_known_fees_execution_latency_product_rules_and_finite_horizon()
  actions = enumerate_explicit_supported_actions_including_no_trade()
  for action in actions:
      exact_post_action = apply_to_full_identity_and_cashflow_ledger(action)
      verify_costs_liquidity_assignment_BP_and_loss_constraints(exact_post_action)
      evaluate_on_joint_scenarios_or_heldout_historical_paths(exact_post_action)
  compare_net_EV_then_qualified_P50_without_resetting_campaign_target()
  publish_uncertainty_and_search_support_not_a_global_optimum_claim()

FINAL OUTPUT:

  atlas = export_atlas_with_methodology_coverage_and_lineage(all_results)
  save_reproducibility_manifest_and_test_report()
  assert(no_trading_orders_sent_and_no_raw_source_modified())

Deliverables are generated by the future implementation. This TXT is their design,
not evidence that those deliverables already exist.

D01. Source audit and missing-data manifest.
     Includes hashes, schemas, duplicates, coverage, missing prerequisites, and

D02. Versioned cohort and historical-universe registry.
     Includes excluded/ineligible/incomplete expirations and listing-evidence limits.

D03. Reproducible fixed-contract panel and moving-chain snapshot store.
     Includes availability, quality masks, identity, source lineage, and clocks.

D04. Descriptive lifecycle atlas and machine-readable aggregate tables.
     Includes every observed strike, not only the forecasting model's support.

D05. Pricing/shape/factor validation pack.
     Includes supported product/input domains, solver errors, reconstruction errors,
     masks, quote-interval conflicts, and training-only representation artifacts.

D06. Chronological prediction benchmark report.
     Includes B0/B1 and candidate B2, all declared folds/variants, calibration,
     uncertainty, tail behavior, coverage, and negative/unsupported findings.

D07. Optional validated joint path model.
     Separate from direct-horizon forecast release; needed for genuine path/policy
     claims, not for the initial descriptive atlas.

D08. Optional execution-aware action evaluation report.
     Includes costs, latency, capacity, ledger, constraints, EV, and clearly defined
     secondary target probability. No auto-trading capability is required.

D09. Reproducibility bundle.
     Config, source/contract hashes, code/environment versions, split manifests,
     experiment registry, tests, run logs, metric definitions, and artifact manifest.

Release labels must be separate dimensions, for example:
  data_population: OBSERVED_UNIVERSE_ONLY | VERIFIED_LISTED_UNIVERSE
  lifecycle_coverage: PARTIAL | COMPLETE_FOR_DECLARED_SCOPE
  description_status: RELEASED | BLOCKED
  prediction_status: DESCRIPTIVE_ONLY | EXPLORATORY | VALIDATED_WITHIN_DOMAIN
  path_status: NOT_IMPLEMENTED | UNVALIDATED | VALIDATED_WITHIN_DOMAIN
  action_status: DISABLED | QUOTED_BENCHMARK_ONLY | VALIDATED_MODEL_EVALUATION

"All strikes researched" means every observed/listed-in-scope strike is accounted
for in the inventory, coverage, and appropriate descriptive outputs. It does not
mean every strike has a precise IV, supported forecast, or executable trade.
Do not use a forecast-support filter to hide the rest of the required population.

28. ACCEPTANCE TEST MATRIX
==============================================================================

Every release-critical test must have a deterministic fixture and expected result.
Synthetic fixtures are allowed ONLY under an unmistakable test-fixture namespace;
they must never contaminate production research tables or reported empirical results.

SOURCE AND IDENTITY

T01. Both named source contracts are read and hashed; missing either blocks adapters.
T02. Duplicate logical source partitions are reported, not silently chosen/merged.
T03. Unsupported/missing required columns produce schema errors, not guessed aliases.
T04. Strike scale ambiguity stops affected normalization; verified scaling is applied
     once and exact identity remains stable across sessions.
T05. IV percent/decimal conversion is recorded once; mixed conventions are rejected.
T06. Calls and puts sharing strike/expiry remain different contracts.
T07. Adjusted root/deliverable/multiplier is not merged into a standard contract.
T08. Underlying spot comes from SOURCE-OHLCV; contradictory embedded Theta spot is
     retained only as a diagnostic.
T09. Known-good raw files are unchanged after audit, caching, and report generation.
T10. The legacy 00:00:00 intraday selector returns the full session where specified.

CLOCKS AND COHORTS

T11. START_LABELED 09:30 full-bar values cannot enter a 09:30 decision; they may enter
     the verified 09:31 completed-bar decision with correct option cutoffs.
T12. END_LABELED/ALREADY_COMPLETED fixtures do not receive an accidental extra delay.
T13. A subminute option event after the decision cutoff is excluded even if its
     floored minute matches a requested row.
T14. Naive NY times are not treated as UTC; timezone-aware source times normalize
     correctly; DST changes alter actual elapsed seconds but not date-gap DTE.
T15. Holidays and early closes use the versioned calendar, not file presence.
T16. A non-session calendar E-4 is flagged, not replaced with the nearest weekday.
T17. A missing file on a valid E-4 session is a data gap, not a holiday/ineligibility.
T18. Four-session-offset cohorts have their own ID and are never pooled as exact
     four-calendar-date cohorts.
T19. 16:00/EOD rows are not silently mixed into canonical 09:30 <= t < 16:00 RTH rows.
T20. Missing terminal evidence leaves the lifecycle boundary unresolved, not filled
     by the last available quote or an unverified 15:59 underlying close.

UNIVERSE AND MISSINGNESS

T21. A later first-seen strike is absent from the t0 observed universe and is added
     to the moving chain only at documented availability.
T22. A verified listed but unquoted contract remains in the listing/coverage panel.
T23. Unknown listed denominator produces null listed-coverage percentage.
T24. A contract losing quotes near expiry remains in the archive and contributes
     missingness, not a zero return or complete-case exclusion hidden from reports.
T25. Zero bid with valid positive ask is retained; zero ask/crossed/NaN quotes are
     treated according to the source contract and preserved for diagnostics.
T26. Unknown quote-update age is not replaced by zero snapshot age.
T27. Same-session oi is unavailable intraday; oi_before is eligible under its
     canonical availability convention.
T28. A full-session partition-integrity check does not demand that every strike
     quote in 98% of minutes or fabricate individual missing observations.

FEATURES, PRICING, AND SHAPES

T29. Adding future underlying rows does not change an earlier feature/snapshot.
T30. Rolling-ATM and fixed-original-ATM contract returns demonstrably differ when
     spot crosses strikes; the software does not silently swap identities.
T31. Missing forward inputs keep k null while supported spot-moneyness continues.
T32. tau=0 invokes terminal/payoff logic and never IV inversion or division by zero.
T33. A low-vega/flat exercise-value fixture yields nonidentified IV intervals rather
     than a false precise number.
T34. American solver convergence and independent benchmark tests pass declared
     tolerances; exercise and dividend boundaries are explicitly exercised in tests.
T35. Monotone/convex strike constraints hold where declared; negative-rate fixtures
     do not use an invalid fixed slope bound of one.
T36. Conflicting bid/ask intervals generate visible slacks/status; raw quotes remain
     unchanged and no artificial arbitrage-profit claim is produced.
T37. Large missing strike gaps and out-of-support wings remain unsupported even
     inside a broad outer strike hull.
T38. A factor model with good ATM fit but unacceptable wing errors fails broad-domain
     support; raw wing observations still appear in descriptive outputs.
T39. Fixed K is re-mapped after spot/forward changes before reconstructing outcomes.
T40. Vendor Greek units cannot silently mix with recomputed annualized sensitivities.

LABELS, DEPENDENCE, AND VALIDATION

T41. Requested horizon exceeding the session is not silently shortened or moved
     across an overnight gap in the same-session profile.
T42. A missing requested endpoint is not replaced with the next future valid quote.
T43. An observed sampled hit followed by a loss remains a hit for that defined
     first-hit label, but not necessarily a realized execution success.
T44. Missing-grid first-hit bounds include unresolved cases; complete minute data
     is not falsely labeled continuous monitoring.
T45. Independent leg highs do not generate a combo target hit without a compatible
     simultaneous portfolio observation.
T46. Random row split and lifecycle overlap across train/test trigger validation
     errors. Actual overlapping label intervals are purged.
T47. Future-fitted scalers/factors/clusters cannot be loaded into a past fold.
T48. Training labels that mature after a training cutoff are excluded.
T49. Calendar-block resampling moves all co-occurring chains/strikes together;
     strikingly larger quote counts do not manufacture narrower independent-row CIs.
T50. Hyperparameter/test leakage is detectable through experiment/split manifests;
     a consumed final test is not relabeled untouched after redesign.
T51. Independent marginal scenario sampling fails the joint-model interface unless
     explicitly used as a diagnostic negative-control model.
T52. Out-of-support query states return a named unsupported result, not extrapolated
     high-confidence probabilities.
T53. A no-signal synthetic fixture does not systematically pass an uncorrected
     multiple-search "profitable discovery" gate.
T54. A descriptive-only model can be released honestly when predictive gates fail.

EXECUTION, ACCOUNTING, AND REPRODUCIBILITY

T55. Fees/slippage are counted exactly once; long/short entry and exit quote sides
     have the correct sign, multiplier, quantity, and latency.
T56. A close based on a completed final regular bar does not obtain a fictional
     fill inside an already closed session.
T57. Opening cash credit is not counted as realized profit while liabilities remain.
T58. Rolls preserve original target and cumulative ledger; infinite-max-profit
     structures have P50-of-max status NOT_APPLICABLE.
T59. An unmodeled early assignment or unsupported longer-DTE roll blocks applicable
     account/policy results instead of pretending the risk cap is guaranteed.
T60. Same frozen inputs/config/seed reproduce outputs within declared deterministic
     tolerance; parallelism does not alter contract population or time semantics.
T61. Source modification invalidates the affected cache; learned caches cannot be
     reused across incompatible folds/training cutoffs.
T62. Resource limits, restart checkpoints, atomic writes, and partial-task recovery
     work without producing complete-looking partial artifacts.

Additional tests should be added for discovered source quirks. Never weaken these
checks merely because a first implementation otherwise fails its release gate.

29. IMPLEMENTATION MILESTONES AND EXIT CRITERIA
==============================================================================

M0. SPECIFICATION AND SOURCE AUDIT
  Read the two required contracts; inspect actual local catalog/schema examples;
  establish clock, calendar, product metadata, and actual coverage evidence.
  Deliver D01 and a list of blocked prerequisites.
  Exit: no guessed data layout, source authority, or timestamp convention.

M1. IDENTITY, CALENDAR, COHORTS, AND UNIVERSES
  Implement canonical contract IDs, entry definitions, listing/observed sets,
  scheduled observation grids, terminal evidence status, and missing-data registry.
  Deliver D02. Pass source/identity/cohort tests.
  Exit: an independent engineer can enumerate included and excluded expirations
        and explain every contract's membership without inspecting model output.

M2. CAUSAL PANELS AND DATA QUALITY
  Implement validation, completed-bar snapshots, authoritative underlying joins,
  prior OI/context availability, masks, source lineage, and immutable derivatives.
  Deliver D03 with one small auditable real-data sample plus full coverage tables.
  Exit: no-lookahead and missingness tests pass before heavy modeling begins.

M3. DESCRIPTIVE LIFECYCLE ATLAS
  Implement registered estimands, initial/current coordinate views, price/spread/
  availability distributions, weighting, dependence-aware uncertainty, and support.
  Deliver initial D04 without waiting for a sophisticated forecast model.
  Exit: all observed strikes accounted for; no complete-case survivorship hidden;
        charts/tables distinguish actual observations from estimates.

M4. PRICING AND JOINT SHAPE REPRESENTATION
  Add supported American pricing/IV intervals, constrained fits, training-only
  functional factors, reconstruction and tail-region diagnostics.
  Deliver D05. Raw price outputs remain available where IV inputs are missing.
  Exit: supported mathematical domain and measured numerical/reconstruction errors
        are explicit; a low-dimensional model is not assumed adequate everywhere.

M5. FORECAST BENCHMARKS
  Freeze chronological splits; implement B0/B1, then B2; generate separately tested
  labels; compare proper scores and calibration using calendar-block uncertainty.
  Deliver development portion of D06 and an experiment registry.
  Exit: all candidates compared on matched support; no random split or leaked basis.

M6. SEALED VALIDATION AND RESEARCH RELEASE
  Freeze selected specification; evaluate the final test once; publish positive,
  negative, sparse, and unsupported results with clear release labels.
  Deliver final D04/D06/D09.
  Exit: reproducible description and an honest forecast-support conclusion, whether
        or not any predictive edge survives.

M7. OPTIONAL JOINT PATH EXTENSION
  Implement PATH-A or PATH-B only when needed; test multi-step and first-hit behavior,
  observation/closure effects, tail dependence, and expiry boundary handling.
  Deliver D07.
  Exit: path claims are validated independently of direct endpoint predictions.

M8. OPTIONAL EXECUTION-AWARE DECISION RESEARCH
  Add explicit actions, realistic quoted/fill models, ledger, fee/capacity/assignment
  handling, constraints, and EV-first/P50-second comparisons.
  Deliver D08 without automatic order submission.
  Exit: any claimed advantage survives costs, risk checks, chronological evaluation,
        and uncertainty; otherwise publish that it does not.

Do not skip M0-M3 to train a large model immediately. M7/M8 are not prerequisites
for completing the core whole-chain descriptive research requested here.

30. SMALL EXAMPLES THAT DEFINE THE REQUIRED LOGIC
==============================================================================

These are hypothetical unit-test examples, not QQQ observations or trading advice.

EXAMPLE A: a disappearing quote
  A put is observed at the cohort start, becomes far OTM, and stops appearing later.
  Correct: keep its identity and earlier path; later samples are missing/unquoted
           according to evidence. Terminal payoff only when separately verified.
  Incorrect: delete the contract or set all later quotes to zero.

EXAMPLE B: new strike after entry
  Strike K_new is first observed on the 2-DTE day.
  Correct: include its observed path and late-appearance flag in the moving chain;
           no hypothetical 4-DTE entry unless prior listing and price are verified.
  Incorrect: use the final union of strikes as the opening tradable universe.

EXAMPLE C: a moving ATM label
  Spot rises from near K1 to near K2 while the original call remains at K1.
  Correct: fixed-contract analysis follows K1; current-ATM description may use K2.
  Incorrect: splice K1 and K2 prices into a buy-and-hold return without trades.

EXAMPLE D: whole-chain dependence
  One market move changes 600 contracts at the same timestamp.
  Correct: 600 related observations within one contemporaneous market event.
  Incorrect: 600 independent experiments establishing a tiny standard error.

EXAMPLE E: price uncertainty becomes IV uncertainty
  An option has a narrow dollar quote interval but very low vega.
  Correct: report a possibly wide compatible IV interval and emphasize price-space
           behavior; retain the strike even if the IV factor model excludes it.
  Incorrect: treat one midpoint IV as an exact observation or delete its raw history.

EXAMPLE F: observed midpoint touch is not a fill
  A spread's theoretical midpoint reaches a profit target and then reverses.
  Correct: sampled midpoint-hit label can be true; realized target status depends
           on the declared order/fill model and costs.
  Incorrect: report the target as realized automatically at the favorable midpoint.

EXAMPLE G: a calendar cohort that does not exist
  E_date minus four calendar dates falls on a non-session date.
  Correct: preserve the expiration in the registry with NO_EXACT_CALENDAR_4_SESSION;
           optionally study a separate four-session-offset profile.
  Incorrect: silently move entry and mix different definitions under one label.

EXAMPLE H: a price fit does not establish EV
  A pricing model reproduces today's chain extremely accurately.
  Correct: evaluate its historical conditional dynamics on unseen data before
           using its forecasts for EV.
  Incorrect: conclude that its risk-neutral probabilities are actual return odds.

EXAMPLE I: midprices versus quote-side costs
  Hypothetical long entry quote is 1.00/1.10; later quote is 1.02/1.12.
  Midpoint change is +0.02 per quoted unit.
  Quoted-side round trip buys at 1.10 and sells at 1.02: -0.08 per unit before fees.
  For a verified 100 multiplier and one contract, these are +USD 2 theoretical
  versus -USD 8 quoted-side, not the same "profitable outcome."

EXAMPLE J: present-data geometry is not aging causality
  A chain's put wing becomes more expensive between its 4-DTE and 3-DTE sessions.
  Correct: describe the conditional joint spot/volatility/liquidity/time change.
  Incorrect: attribute it to DTE alone without accounting for the intervening state.

31. LLM IMPLEMENTER HANDOFF AND FINAL DEFINITION OF DONE
==============================================================================

When implementing this plan, the LLM must:
  1. Read the two named data contracts and this document, then inspect actual data.
  2. Restate resolved conventions in a machine-readable run configuration.
  3. Mark missing evidence explicitly instead of asking the model to invent it.
  4. Implement milestones in order, with tests before dependent calculations.
  5. Keep actual data, estimates, forecasts, and synthetic test fixtures distinct.
  6. Make every mathematical output traceable to its estimand, units, timestamps,
     source rows, model/fold, assumptions, and support domain.
  7. Never present this design as an implemented or validated historical finding.
  8. Avoid unrelated broker-account integration or automatic trading functionality.

The core research system is done when an independent reviewer can:
  - Rebuild the same archive inventory and eligible expiration cohorts.
  - See every observed strike accounted for and any unknown listed-universe gaps.
  - Trace fixed-ID paths from 4 DTE toward expiry without hindsight-based selection.
  - Inspect synchronized chain snapshots with explicit availability and missingness.
  - Understand initial versus current coordinates and the clock definitions.
  - Read a descriptive atlas showing behavior, distributions, uncertainty, and gaps.
  - Reproduce the equations, aggregation weights, coverage denominators, and labels.
  - Verify the American/terminal conventions used by any price/IV calculation.
  - See chronological forecast comparisons that do not rely on leaked information.
  - Distinguish "market pattern observed" from "forecast validated" and from
    "cost-adjusted trading advantage established."
  - Re-run the same versioned pipeline and obtain the same conclusions within the
    declared numerical tolerances, without modifying the canonical datasets.

Failure to find a profitable trading rule does not invalidate a correctly performed
market-behavior study. Failure to obtain all historical listed contracts DOES limit
the population claim and must remain visible. Precision cannot be manufactured by
more model complexity, more correlated rows, or unsupported extrapolation.
